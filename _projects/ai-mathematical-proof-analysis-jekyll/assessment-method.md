# Proof of Progress: best-current assessment, version 1.0.0

This is a revisable AI assessment of human-comparison challenge, separate from
the unchanged strict historical policy, AI contribution and proof verification.
It is not an expert consensus, a calibrated probability or a correctness verdict.

## Comparator and categories

The default comparator is a relevant specialist or small team using methods
available immediately before the report, over an ordinary research project
(weeks to months). It is not a comparison with the model's wall-clock runtime.
Individual records state narrower comparisons where the source supports them.

- Expected: plausibly within normal specialist-human research. This includes
  useful technical improvements and applications of existing methods; it does
  not mean trivial, unimportant or previously known.
- Difficult: substantial non-routine progress or a barrier requiring exceptional
  insight/effort, but plausibly human-achievable.
- Superhuman: a credible task-specific crossing of the previous human frontier
  in capability, speed or scale. A historical-frontier judgment is distinct from
  a matched human-time experiment. Age alone, failed model trials, prestige and
  proof length are not sufficient. All initial Superhuman estimates are low
  confidence; no matched human-time comparison was established by this pass.
- Unassessed: insufficient concrete detail for a useful estimate. This is not
  Expected, and it remains in the denominator and a neutral chart band.

Each record has a likely category, qualitative confidence, a reason, plausible
alternatives, a comparator, source IDs, source hash, method and assessment date.
High confidence concerns this judgment only. Medium/low confidence is shown as
provisional; proof checks have their own unchanged evidence fields.

## Initial assessment and repeatable triage

All 1,239 claims were processed with the same versioned rules, including the
historical months. Five hundred received individual AI readings of retained
source excerpts: all 71 previous Superhuman/possible-Superhuman claims, a
purposive 24-record calibration sample, and all 405 ambiguous technical-summary
cases from the preliminary triage. This was not a random prevalence study,
a reopening of every source document, a human review or execution of proofs.

The remaining records use transparent automated triage:

1. A current source-pinned individual judgment takes precedence.
2. A strict Superhuman label alone defaults to a provisional Difficult estimate
   with Superhuman as an alternative until its human comparator is assessed.
3. Recorded exact-target resistance or complete settlement of a prior target
   supports Difficult, usually medium confidence, without inventing human hours.
4. Explicit settlement/breakthrough language supports low-confidence Difficult,
   taking precedence over the material-extension shortcut below.
5. A coded narrow/material extension without recorded resistance supports a
   low-confidence Expected hypothesis. Missing resistance is not coded as zero.
6. Explicit relationships to existing methods, extensions, analogues or bounded
   refinements support low-confidence Expected.
7. A technical result without those signals remains Unassessed until an
   individual pass: words such as theorem, proof or bound do not establish effort.
8. Sparse or non-concrete summaries remain Unassessed.

These rules are a fast, imperfect editorial heuristic, not a learned or calibrated
model. In particular, extensions can be very difficult and a technical abstract
alone cannot measure human effort. The source-specific reasons expose which
rule supplied each judgment; later individual readings should replace weak
fallbacks. Never use these categories to fill a desired shape in the graph.

## Counting, contribution and collection

Every selected claim appears once in its most likely tier. Both sets of controls,
the summary and the chart use the same mutually exclusive bands. Lift is
Difficult plus Superhuman divided by all selected claims, including Unassessed.
Filtering categories changes the denominator. Counts are bounded claims, not
equal-sized achievements or a representative measure of human productivity.

AI contribution uses recorded generation-stage allocations, or explicitly
retained source attributions labeled as reported. Human-led/AI-assisting and
unknown allocation are separate. A title, bibliography, conference name or
generic model mention is not evidence of a decisive AI contribution.

Collection dates are earliest supported observations in retained evidence,
not guaranteed first-ever discoveries. Backfill means collection month exceeds
report month. Date uncertainty takes precedence for unresolved submission,
discovery or first-observed qualifying-revision dates. The 519-source baseline
has no reconstructed per-source first-collection date; its migration access
dates must not be used. The Collection chart keeps claims in their report month
and colours their collection timing. The source ledger's dates remain unchanged.

## Refresh, selective review and revisions

Every build runs the assessment generator before the hero and website build.
New records receive triage automatically. Source-pinned individual judgments
are invalidated when their source record changes and enter the re-review queue.
Do not silently re-pin an old rationale to changed evidence.

Daily editorial refreshes prioritize apparent breakthroughs, disputed cases,
changed sources and doubtful AI attribution; they also inspect a rotating
ordinary-result sample when source retrieval allows. The queue marks work
pending, never work completed. Its ordinary sample is selected deterministically
by a hash of record ID and UTC week, at a 5% selection rate. This is a review
allocation mechanism, not an estimate of population error.

Keep individual judgments and per-source collection provenance as authoring
inputs; regenerate all public assessment files. Preserve category/confidence
revision history. A rubric change must recalculate all historical months, not
only the newest month. Report any unresolved checks and do not delay unrelated
records. Never conflate this layer's provisional Superhuman estimate with the
strict policy's gated Superhuman assignment.
